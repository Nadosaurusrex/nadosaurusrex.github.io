I don’t believe talent is rare. I believe access is.

I love AI not because it’s intelligent, fast, or magical.

I love it because for the first time there is something tangible you can hand someone and say: this can change what is possible for you.

For most of history, learning, building, and creating was constrained. By money. By credentials. By language. By social norms designed for a very specific type of person.

If you were too slow for rigid processes, too unconventional for professional labels, unable to articulate yourself in the moment, neurodivergent, poor, not a native speaker, or simply unlucky, you were misaligned with the system.

AI breaks this alignment requirement.

If you can read and write, you can learn to use AI. And once you do, you gain range. You gain tools. You gain a way to compensate for what the world decided you were lacking.

AI takes 20 minutes and a real problem, instead of the years demanded by traditional paths.

Like writing an email to a government office in a language that isn’t yours, and you’re fairly sure they’re ignoring you because your grammar makes you sound like a child asking for permission instead of a citizen asking for what they’re owed.

Like turning your messy 2 AM idea into something clean enough that your boss doesn’t think you’re speaking in riddles.

Like being slow with software, not because you’re incapable, but because you actually read what the buttons do before clicking them, and being penalized for it while the fast clickers break things with confidence and get promoted anyway.

Like finally keeping up in meetings when you’re the person who thinks slowly, processes deeply, and always has the right answer three hours later, when it no longer matters because everyone already decided you weren’t fast enough to contribute.

This matters most for people with low income and low margin for error: people who can’t afford years of education, people who can’t fail publicly more than once, people who don’t have networks to cushion mistakes, people who are intelligent but never had the right interface.

AI builds the bridge that was missing.

Since power is being democratized, and intelligence is not scarce, and tools once reserved for institutions are becoming usable by individuals, my job is to put these tools in the hands of those who have been forgotten. People who were never set up to succeed.

I want to teach and show that:

> If you can read, you can learn.
>
> And if you can learn, you’re not stuck.

You don’t have to be a genius. You don’t need perfect communication skills. You don’t need the right accent, the right background, or the right personality.

You need mastery. And someone to show you how to turn it into leverage.

I want to go where learning is needed and will be reshaped in the near future: schools, streets, side projects, messy paths, nonlinear lives.

Give people tools and say: try this, it works.

Because we’ve always been told the future is decided by whoever is smartest, but in reality it’s decided by whoever gets access first, clearly, and without shame.

And if AI is rewriting how the world works, then my responsibility is to make sure the rewriting includes the people it usually forgets.

Nadir
