_"Intelligente ma non si applica."_ Intelligent, but doesn't apply himself. My teachers said it about me all through primary school, and I did the minimum to pass. Nobody said "dyslexia" until high school.

I hear that sentence again now, about AI. A lot of people are worried that it makes you stupid. That you don't really learn anything with it. That it's cheating. Intelligent, but doesn't apply himself, said this time about everyone with a phone.

I notice it's said mostly by people who always had another way in, and that alone doesn't make them wrong. The long way worked for them. School that fit. Books in their language. Someone at the table. When you have always had a way in, it feels like your own effort. **From the inside, access is invisible.** So anyone else's way in looks like a shortcut, and a shortcut looks like cheating. My teachers couldn't see what I was missing, and plenty of people can't see it in a kid with AI now.

I want to be fair to the worry. For the people saying it, it can be true: let the machine do your thinking and you lose something real.

Then there are the people who never had any of it. Slow, second language, no money for the long way, told they're not the type. I don't believe they are less intelligent. I believe nobody ever sat with them. **Those are the hands I want AI in.** For them it's the first patient thing that ever explained anything twice, and they're the first to be told it's cheating.

The sentence blames the kid for everything the adults never gave him: a name for the problem, a person with time, a second try. From the outside, having no access looks exactly like not trying. And using the only patient thing you were ever given looks exactly like cheating.

I know this because nothing about me ever changed.

At home my mother sat next to me with a _mestolo_, the wooden spoon, and stayed until I learned the thing. In the first year of my engineering degree I nearly had to leave. Someone gave me a schedule and shared their notes, and I finished. Both times a person handed me the way in.

It was never me trying harder.

After 2022 I learned to code with AI, with nobody in the next chair, which worked only because by then I could read English and liked this stuff already.

AI has my mother's patience. It sits next to you for as long as it takes, repeats itself a hundred times and never gets tired. What it doesn't have is the spoon. It will not come and get you, it will not ask what your real problem is, and it will not insist until the thing goes in. Ask it to do the whole job for you and it will. So the worried people have a point.

**Either the machine does the work for you, or it sits next to you until you can do the work yourself.** The machine doesn't choose between the two.

The person holding the spoon does.

My mother did that for me. Now it's ours to do, with an AI open on the table. Before you say cheating, or brain rot, about someone, **ask whether anybody ever sat with them.** Then sit down and make it the second kind: their problem, in their words, you steer, and you don't leave. Choose the person you would have skipped, the one you decided long ago wouldn't apply themselves.

I looked like that once.

I still think this is my job. At work I get to do it for companies, and I'm grateful that's what I'm paid for. Outside work I want to do it for the people nobody sits with: put the thing in front of them, and hold the spoon until they don't need me.

A boy goes back to school in September, and a teacher will probably say it about him this year: intelligent, but doesn't apply himself. He already has AI in his pocket, and the one thing school has taught him about it is the word cheating. So this last part is for him.

They're right about the first half: you are intelligent. The second half is wrong. What looks like not trying is a list of things nobody gave you yet: a name for the problem, someone with time, a second try. The machine in your pocket can give you some of them, and it will never once call you slow. Make it explain, twice, ten times, until you can do the thing without it. **That is applying yourself.** I hope an adult near you picks up the spoon. Until one does, you're allowed to pick it up yourself.
